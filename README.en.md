# TicTag — What if emails had a useful lifetime?

**One simple tag to make inbox cleanup easier. An idea to share worldwide.**

[Français](README.md) · [Website](https://tictag.green)

An expired promotion, an old notification, a confirmation code you no longer need… Why keep these emails indefinitely? And why review them one by one to clear them out?

TicTag is a text convention for suggesting an email’s useful lifetime: **1, 3 or 5 years**. A tag in the body or signature helps recipients find older messages and review them for deletion.

The ambition is to make this shared reference an international standard, as easy to pass on as a hashtag. The aim is to reduce unnecessary storage of obsolete emails and its associated environmental impact.

## One line to get started

Choose one of these lines:

```text
TicTag1Y – This email is biodegradable. Learn more: https://tictag.green/info
TicTag3Y – This email is biodegradable. Learn more: https://tictag.green/info
TicTag5Y – This email is biodegradable. Learn more: https://tictag.green/info
```

TicTag identifies the convention, the number indicates the recommended period, and **Y** stands for “Year”. Choose a period based on the actual content, validity and retention needs. Some messages should remain untagged.

## The real opportunity: automated emails

Anyone can tag an individual email. But TicTag is primarily designed for **automated email templates with a limited useful lifetime**: newsletters, notifications, shipping updates and more.

The sender adds the tag once to a template. Future messages generated from that template then carry it, whether there are thousands or millions of sends.

**Update one template today. Make tomorrow’s emails easier to sort.**

No dedicated software or infrastructure is needed to add the text to existing communications.

## What does the recipient do?

Nothing on arrival: the information is simply there.

When it is time to clean up, the recipient searches for **TicTag1Y**, filters for messages older than one year, reviews the results and deletes what is no longer needed. The same principle applies to the three- and five-year tags.

**The recipient always decides. TicTag never deletes anything automatically.**

Search filters and conversation handling vary between email clients. See the [practical guide](docs/guide-pratique.md).

## Why “This email is biodegradable”?

Because it sparks curiosity. A recipient clicks the link, discovers the idea and may adopt it too.

“Biodegradable” is a metaphor: the email does not decompose or erase itself. It carries a reference that helps people decide when to delete it.

**Every tagged email can also help spread the idea.**

The link to **https://tictag.green/info** is central to this process: recipients discover the explanation and can adopt TicTag themselves. Keep it alongside the tag in your templates.

Never translate **TicTag1Y**, **TicTag3Y** or **TicTag5Y**. The accompanying sentence can be translated, while the keyword stays the same across languages.

## Already in use

According to information supplied by the project founder, TicTag is already used in real communications across numerous e-commerce websites, associations and public administrations.

The convention is defined and operational. See the [usage examples](docs/exemples-usages.md), without publishing organisation names or internal assessment spreadsheets.

## A simple convention, sometimes specific decisions

Adding a line is easy. Choosing periods across a company’s templates can require closer review: attachments, offer validity, warranties, mixed-purpose messages and active cases.

The support service envisaged around TicTag focuses on inventorying templates, choosing tags, handling exceptions, helping with integration and maintaining those decisions.

The founder intends tagging organisations’ own emails to be free. See the [usage and licensing status](LICENCE-ET-USAGES.md) for the announced licence and the scope that remains to be clarified.

## A word to make popular

You can start adding the text today, without building an app or obtaining prior agreement from an email provider.

The next step is to share the idea and encourage widespread adoption. Becoming an international standard is an ambition to build together.

**Try it on one email template. Share the idea. Help make TicTag a habit.**

Future development focuses on plugins and cleanup systems, including automatic deletion explicitly enabled by the recipient. These tools are not yet supplied by this repository. The website and video support communication; the convention already works independently of them.
